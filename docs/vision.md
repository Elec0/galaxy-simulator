# Vision and principles

[Project index](../README.md)

## Vision

Create a 2D command game about participating in a galaxy that continues to operate without the player. The player owns ships and gives them orders through a map rather than directly piloting them.

The game should preserve the appeal of starting small. A player may operate one ship for an entire game, build a specialized fleet, control an industrial network, or eventually become a major political power.

## Core principles

### Material causality

Ships, station modules, and other major assets should require resources and production capacity. A faction's losses and logistical problems should affect what it can build and accomplish.

### Autonomous galaxy

Factions, traders, stations, and ships pursue their own goals. The simulation does not wait for the player and should remain interesting when observed without intervention.

### Legibility

The player should be able to investigate why something happened: what a station needs, why a ship chose a route, why a faction ordered a fleet, or where a shortage began.

### One command model

The same map-based interaction should work whether the player owns one ship or hundreds. Expansion should add strategic possibilities without replacing the original game with a separate control scheme.

### Simulation before spectacle

Simple symbols, overlays, movement, and effects are sufficient. Visual work should clarify the state of the simulation rather than obscure it.

### Tunable pacing

Travel, extraction, manufacturing, construction, and strategic decisions use configurable simulation-time values. Gameplay pacing remains separate from the computer's simulation throughput. See [Time and pacing](time-and-pacing.md).

## Non-goals for the first version

- Direct first-person or arcade-style ship piloting
- A detailed 3D environment
- Complex narrative campaigns
- Crew-level or population-level simulation
- Large commodity and technology catalogs before the core economy works

See [Player experience](player-experience.md) for how these principles appear during play.
